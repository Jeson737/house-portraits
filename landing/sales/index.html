The problem

Name the pain before the cure

FAQ

The questions that stop people from buying

Answered here so the page, not a salesperson, handles them. The same answers feed a FAQ rich result in search.

When should a landing page be long instead of short?

When the decision is expensive, unfamiliar or emotional: a course, a service, a considered purchase. Short pages work for offers people already understand. Length is not the goal; answering every question before it is asked is.

Does anyone read a page this long?

Buyers do, and skimmers scan the headlines. That is why every section here has a title that carries the argument on its own. Read only the H2s and you still get the pitch.

How many calls to action should a sales page have?

One offer, repeated. This page asks for the same action after the hero, after the proof and at the end. Different offers on one page split attention and lower conversion.

Where do price and guarantee go?

After the value is clear and the proof has landed. Price before benefits feels expensive; a guarantee right after the price removes the last excuse to wait.

How do I adapt this page to my product with AstroWind?

Every block is a widget in src/components/widgets (Hero, Content, Features, Steps2, Stats, Testimonials, Pricing, FAQs, CallToAction). Keep the order, replace the copy and images, wire the buttons to your checkout or form.

Are the numbers and testimonials on this page real?

The numbers are constraints, not results, and the testimonials are labelled placeholders. Use only real customer quotes and measured results on your own page; invented proof is the fastest way to lose the sale.
